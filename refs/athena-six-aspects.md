# ATHENA'NIN ALTI YÜZÜ / THE SIX ASPECTS OF ATHENA
### Interactive projection, İzmir Archaeology Museum
**v1 — 13 September 2026 — for review with Daniele**

---

## The object

Athena, Ephesos (Vedius Gymnasion). Inv. 000.011.
H. 1.84 m / W. 0.55 m / D. 0.33 m. Grey-white medium-grained marble. Early Imperial Period.
Vedius Gymnasium built 147–149 CE by P. Vedius Antoninus and Flavia Papiana.

Physical facts used in the texts below:
- Back roughly worked — made to stand against a wall or in a niche.
- Head is a separate piece, reattached, visible on a metal pin.
- Face heavily worn.
- Corinthian helmet pushed back on the head, not lowered over the face.
- Aegis present and intact: scaled surface, winged gorgoneion, coiled snakes at the edge.
- Right arm missing from the shoulder, left arm from the elbow, both feet from the ankles.
- Reconstructed composition: spear in the right hand, shield on the left.
- Three garments (chiton, himation, kolpos folds) are the most carefully carved element.

Open question for the museum: the statue is catalogued Early Imperial, but the building is 147–149 CE. Is the statue older than the gymnasium it was found in?

---

## Structure

Six icons, three to the left and three to the right of the statue. Visitor selects by hand (dwell to select). Selected icon expands to fill the projection area. A short supporting video loops on one side while the sentences appear one by one on the other. 20–30 s, then return to the menu.

Each section is anchored to a physical feature of this specific statue. The last sentence of each section is that anchor.

| # | Section | Epithet | Anchor on the statue | State |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | DOĞUŞ / BIRTH | Metis | metal pin at the neck, reattached head | visible repair |
| 2 | SAVAŞ / WAR | Promachos | aegis, pushed-back Corinthian helmet | intact |
| 3 | ZANAAT / CRAFT | Ergane | three layers of cloth | intact, most detailed |
| 4 | BAYKUŞ / THE OWL | coin and trade | owl + olive on the Athenian tetradrachm | context |
| 5 | AKIL / MIND | mētis | both arms missing | absent |
| 6 | GYMNASION | paideia | where the statue came from | context |

## 1. DOĞUŞ / BIRTH
*Metis*

**TR**

Zeus, kendisinden güçlü bir çocuk doğuracağı söylenen Metis'i yuttu.

Athena babasının başından, zırhıyla birlikte, yetişkin olarak doğdu.

Annesi yoktu. Aklı sonradan öğrenilmedi, onunla birlikte geldi.

**EN**

Zeus swallowed Metis, warned that she would bear a child greater than him.

Athena was born from her father's head, fully grown and already armoured.

She had no mother. Her intelligence was not learned. It arrived with her.

**Video.** A single crack opening very slowly across a marble forehead, light leaking from inside. Nothing emerges. 25 s, loopable.

**Optional 4th sentence.** "Bu heykelin başı da gövdesinden ayrıdır." / "The head of this statue is also a separate piece." Points the visitor directly at the object in front of them. Needs museum approval — it draws attention to the restoration.

## 2. SAVAŞ / WAR
*Promachos*

**TR**

Ares savaşın öfkesidir, Athena savaşın aklıdır.

Göğsündeki aigis'te Medusa'nın başı vardır; saldırmak için değil, uzak tutmak için.

Ona Promachos derlerdi: en önde duran.

Miğferi yüzüne indirilmemiş, başına itilmiştir. Savaşta değil, nöbettedir.

**EN**

Ares is the fury of war. Athena is its intelligence.

On her chest the aegis carries the head of Medusa, worn to keep danger away rather than to attack.

They called her Promachos, the one who stands in front.

Her helmet is pushed back, not lowered. She is not fighting. She is watching.

**Video.** Very slow camera drift across the scaled surface of the aegis, coiled snakes at the edge catching light. Almost no movement, all tension.

---

## 3. ZANAAT / CRAFT
*Ergane*

**TR**

Athena dokumacıların, çömlekçilerin ve marangozların tanrıçasıdır.

Ona Ergane, yani işçi derlerdi.

Arakhne dokumada onu yenmeye kalkıştı ve örümceğe dönüştürüldü.

Bu heykelde en özenle işlenmiş şey silahı değil, üst üste binen üç kat kumaşıdır.

**EN**

Athena is the goddess of weavers, potters and carpenters.

They called her Ergane, the worker.

Arachne challenged her at the loom and was turned into a spider.

On this statue the most carefully carved thing is not a weapon. It is three layers of cloth.

**Video.** A shuttle passing back and forth on a loom, thread tightening. Easiest section to loop and to read.

## 4. BAYKUŞ / THE OWL
*Coin and trade*

**TR**

Atina'nın gümüş sikkesinin bir yüzünde Athena'nın miğferli başı, diğer yüzünde baykuşu ve bir zeytin dalı vardı.

Bu sikke yaklaşık iki yüzyıl boyunca Akdeniz'in ortak parasıydı.

Tüccarlar ona kısaca "baykuş" derdi.

Athena'nın yüzü, antik dünyada en çok çoğaltılan görüntülerden biridir.

**EN**

One side of Athens' silver coin carried Athena's helmeted head. The other carried her owl and an olive sprig.

For roughly two centuries it was the common currency of the Mediterranean.

Traders simply called it "the owl".

Athena's face is among the most reproduced images of the ancient world.

**Video.** A hand tosses the coin into the air. It turns in slow motion so both faces are legible. The hand catches it. 25 s, cut so the end joins the beginning.

**Check before printing.** The research dossier claims "over 15 million minted". Source unverified. The sentences above deliberately avoid a number.

---

## 5. AKIL / MIND
*Mētis*

**TR**

Athena'nın gücü kaba kuvvet değil, mētis'tir: kurnaz ve pratik akıl.

İlk gemiyi yapanlara, atı ilk dizginleyenlere, çamuru ilk çevirenlere yol gösteren odur.

Odysseus'u yirmi yıl boyunca o yönetti. İkisi de zekâyla kazanır.

Bu heykelde iki kol da eksiktir. Yapan eller kayıp olan kısım.

**EN**

Athena's power is not force. It is mētis: cunning, practical intelligence.

She guided those who built the first ships, who first bridled the horse, who first turned the clay.

She steered Odysseus for twenty years. Both of them win by thinking.

On this statue both arms are missing. The hands that make are the part that is lost.

**Video.** A hand tying a knot, or a bridle being fastened. One action, close up, returns to the start when complete.

**Note.** Sections 1 and 5 are deliberately linked. Metis is the mother's name; mētis is the faculty. Leave the connection for the visitor to make. Do not explain it on the wall.

---

## 6. GYMNASION
*Where this statue came from*

**TR**

Bu heykel Ephesos'ta bir gymnasionda duruyordu.

Gymnasion yalnızca bir spor alanı değildi; gençler orada bedenlerini ve zihinlerini birlikte eğitirdi.

Athena oraya öğrenmenin tanrıçası olarak konmuştu.

Arkası kabaca işlenmiştir, çünkü bir duvarın önünde durmak üzere yapılmıştı.

**EN**

This statue once stood in a gymnasium in Ephesus.

A gymnasium was more than a place for sport. Young men trained body and mind there together.

Athena was placed there as the goddess of learning.

Its back is left rough, because it was made to stand against a wall.

**Video.** The plan of the palaestra assembling line by line, colonnades settling into place. An architectural diagram may work better here than a video.

**Note.** The last sentence closes the whole piece. The visitor is standing in front of that wall, and the wall is carrying the projection. Place this section last, or furthest right, in the icon sequence.

## Two open issues

**Too much text.** Four sentences in two languages cannot be read in 25 s from 2 m. Three options: cut to three sentences per section; set Turkish large and English small as a paired block; or bring the sentences in one at a time, roughly 7 s apart. The third is recommended — it reads, and it gives the video a rhythm to sit against. The fourth sentences (the ones anchored to the statue) are worth keeping; they are what stops the piece being generic.

**Icon system.** All six icons could sit in the same circular frame, i.e. a coin face. Athena's head is the most mass-reproduced coin image of the ancient world, so the menu would read as six struck coins. The Aztec / punk graphic language works with that form, and one geometry holds six different themes together. If section 4's icon clashes with the frame, use the olive sprig there instead of the owl.

## Production decisions already taken

- The statue is masked out of the projection. Its shadow is not used.
- AI video is a supporting element, not the main content: short, single-action, loopable.
- No photogrammetry. Conventional projection, simple interaction, strong graphics.
- No QR. This system is independent of the museum's existing e-müze record.
- Deadline: mid-October 2026.
